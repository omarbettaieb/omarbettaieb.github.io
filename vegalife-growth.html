Strategy before design.

What the evidence actually tells us

01 / VOLUME
The video drove more reported purchases

With 50 Meta-attributed purchases against 28 for Image 3, the video had more volume. It also received substantially more spend ($243.22 versus $133.17), so the volume difference is not a fair standalone efficiency comparison.

02 / EFFICIENCY
Image 3 has stronger attributed ROAS

Image 3 reported 4.74× versus 3.93× for video; CPA was close ($4.76 vs $4.86). This supports a hypothesis for further testing, not proof of superior profitability or creative causality.

03 / QUALITY OF SIGNAL
Clicks and tiny samples need caution

The carousel logged 4.04% all-click CTR but just 1 attributed purchase, while Image 2 had 2. Small numbers, different delivery conditions and all-click CTR make simplistic creative rankings unreliable.

My decision framework for the next iteration

These are proposed next-step decisions derived from the data, not a record of past changes made to the campaigns.

01 / VERIFYReconcile attribution with Shopify

Check genuine paid/confirmed orders, returns, COGS and delivery costs in VegaProfit before treating Meta ROAS as real profit.

02 / RETESTCompare video vs static on fair terms

Test the same audience, timeframe, objective, optimization event and comparable budgets. Evaluate purchases and CPA rather than all-click CTR alone.

03 / ITERATEScale cautiously, then refresh angles

If validated contribution margin holds, consider incremental budget increases for reliable variants; iterate with new hooks and monitor performance as spend changes.

Interpretation, not an experiment report: these ad-level observations do not establish statistical significance, uniform audience targeting or identical delivery conditions. The section distinguishes observed account metrics from potential next steps; it does not claim any historical kill/scale actions or verified revenue lift.
